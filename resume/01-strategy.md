# Strategy: one owner, several businesses, and a W-2 search

## The short answer

- **Yes, a resume with five ventures on it can look strange to a big company,** but only when all five are shown at the same weight. A hiring manager reading it doesn't ask "is she qualified?" They ask three other things:
  1. *Will she actually have time for us?*
  2. *Will she get bored and leave in 8 months?*
  3. *Is she a competitor, a vendor, or a conflict of interest?*

  The resume and cover letter need to answer all three before anyone asks.
- **The fix is hierarchy, not hiding.** Put two roles up front (Dr. Contact Lens and Aesthetic Coaching). Those two prove you can do the job. Collapse the other businesses into one compact "Ventures & Ownership" block. Founders who move into operator roles do exactly this.
- **Don't hide that you own these businesses.** You're well known in eye care, your name is on the websites, and LinkedIn and background checks will show it. Getting caught downplaying ownership does far more damage than owning it confidently. The story to tell: *"I build the systems, hire the team, and step back. The businesses run without me in the chair. What I love doing, and what I'm best at, is the digital work itself."*

## Westchester Vision Care: owner, not "currently seeing patients"

You want the resume to show you own the practice without suggesting you're in clinic every day. Here's how to do that honestly:

- **Title:** `Owner` (not "Owner & Optometrist", not "Full-Scope Optometrist").
- **Bullets:** Write only about building the business: brand positioning in luxury frames, launching the dry-eye service line, website and SEO, patient acquisition, staffing and systems. Leave out clinical duties.
- **License:** Show it once, in a "Licenses & Credentials" line: `Doctor of Optometry (OD), [School]; Licensed Optometrist, California`. It's a credibility asset. In a healthcare company's eyes, a clinician who also builds websites is very rare.
- **The one rule:** Never write anything false, such as "transitioned out of clinical practice", unless it's true. If you take a W-2 job, *make* it true: bring in an associate OD or a per-diem doctor to cover your clinic days. Then in the interview you can say, *"The practice is staffed clinically. My role there is ownership and growth."*
- **If they ask directly** ("Are you still seeing patients?"), answer with the truth and the plan: *"A [day] a week right now. I'm transitioning that to an associate as I take on something like this."*

## Should you wait until after the Dr. Contact Lens exit?

**My recommendation: build both versions now, use the pre-exit version for Opus, and only list the acquisition after it closes.**

Why:

1. **An LOI isn't an exit.** Deals fall apart or get repriced in diligence all the time. "Acquired by Visibly" on a resume before close is a misstatement. It could also **break the LOI's confidentiality clause.** Most LOIs prohibit disclosing the deal. Check yours before you say anything about it to anyone, Opus included.
2. **The acquisition agreement may decide what jobs you can take.** This is the big one. You're in California. California voids most employee non-competes (Bus. & Prof. Code §16600), **but non-competes signed as part of selling a business are enforceable** (§16601). If Visibly's purchase agreement includes a non-compete, a non-solicit or a founder transition/consulting period, a job at another eye-care company like Opus could be restricted. **Ask your deal attorney now:** *"Would a W-2 role at [Opus] fall under the restrictive covenants or the transition obligations in this deal?"* Then negotiate the scope before you sign.
3. **The post-exit story is much stronger,** and it's only ~6 weeks away. "Co-founded, scaled to 1,000+ practices and [$300M+] in sales, and exited" answers the "why would a founder want this job?" question on its own: you've already had your founder win and now you want focused work without the overhead.
4. **But you don't have to wait to *talk* to Opus.** You know people there. A warm, informal conversation ("I saw the Website Manager role and I'm curious what success looks like in it") costs nothing and tells you whether the job is about output or hours. Apply formally with the pre-exit resume if the posting is going to close. Swap in the post-exit version once the deal closes.

### How tech founders write the exit

Use this format only after close:

> **Dr. Contact Lens** — Co-Founder & Chief Marketing Officer · [2020] – [Dec 2026]
> *Acquired by Visibly, December 2026*

Then the bullets show the scale (practices, sales volume, growth) and *your* part in it. If you stay on for a transition period, the dates run to the end of that period, and you can add a line like "Led marketing and brand integration through the transition."

**Check one number first: is the "$300M in contact lens sales" GMV (sales through the platform) or company revenue?** If it's GMV, the resume has to say so ("$300M+ in contact lens sales facilitated through the platform"). Founders get caught on this in background checks and reference calls, and an acquirer's press release will show the real figure.

## Can you hold more than one W-2 job at once?

**Yes, it's legal in the US.** Plenty of people hold two W-2 jobs. Where it goes wrong is in **contracts and disclosure:**

- **Employment agreements.** Read the offer for exclusivity, "outside activities", moonlighting or conflict-of-interest clauses. Many full-time salaried roles at larger companies require you to disclose outside businesses, and some require approval. You *have* outside businesses, so you'll almost certainly disclose them anyway. Do it up front and get it acknowledged in writing.
- **IP assignment.** Employer agreements often claim work you create. California Labor Code §2870 protects things you build on your own time, with your own equipment, that don't relate to the employer's business. A website-manager job at an eye-care company *does* relate to your eye-care ventures, though, so get your existing businesses carved out in writing.
- **Hidden "overemployment"** (two full-time jobs, neither knowing about the other) isn't illegal by itself. It is a firing offense, and possibly a breach of contract, if either agreement forbids it. Given your public profile in the industry, it would also be very easy to discover. Not worth it.
- **Taxes.** With two W-2s each employer withholds as if it's your only job, so you'll probably be under-withheld. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator and adjust your W-4. Extra Social Security withheld across employers comes back as a credit on your return.

**A better shape for "I'll be everyone's website manager":** that's what **Aesthetic Coaching** already is. One W-2 for stability and the mortgage, plus Aesthetic Coaching taking fractional/retainer clients (1099), scales better and has fewer conflicts than stacking W-2s.

## The home loan: a W-2 may not be the only route (or the fastest)

Talk to a **mortgage broker** (not just one bank) before taking a job for the paystub. General rules of thumb (confirm with the lender, since guidelines vary):

- **A new W-2 job** can often qualify you quickly with an offer letter and ~30 days of paystubs, especially in a related field. Watch probation periods and any pay that is mostly bonus.
- **Second-job W-2 income** usually only counts once there's a ~2-year history of holding both jobs.
- **Self-employment / business income** (K-1s, Schedule C) usually counts with **2 years of tax returns**. You may already qualify on business income.
- **After the exit,** proceeds you receive can serve as **reserves or a large down payment**, and some lenders offer **asset-depletion** or **asset-based** loans that qualify you on liquid assets rather than salary. Earn-out and escrow/holdback money usually doesn't count until it's actually paid.

Bottom line: take the Opus job (or any job) because you want it. Don't take it only because you think a mortgage requires a W-2.

## The "overqualified" objection: answer it before they ask

Say this in the cover letter and the first interview:

> "I've spent [X] years founding and scaling companies. The part of that work I'm consistently best at, and enjoy most, is the digital side: the site, SEO, the conversion funnel, the brand system. My businesses are staffed and running. I'm looking for one focused, long-term role where that's the whole job, at a company I already know and respect."

Back it up with what they're actually afraid of:
- **Time:** "[Opus] would be my only employer. My businesses have operators running day-to-day."
- **Tenure:** "I'm not job-hunting to start something else. I just finished an exit."
- **Conflict:** "Here's the full list of what I own. None of them competes with [Opus], and I'll disclose and recuse from anything that touches [X]." (Check whether Techify's sponsors or Dr. Contact Lens/Visibly overlap with Opus's customers, vendors or competitors. If they do, raise it yourself.)
